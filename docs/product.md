# Product Overview

This document explains **how learning works** inside OpenSen. For positioning, scope, and how we talk about the product to users, see [Product strategy](product-strategy.md).

## What is OpenSen?

**OpenSen** (Open Sentence) is a situational speaking-reflex system for English. A learner picks a real situation they are about to face, builds sentences that fit their own life, and practices until they can say them without translating in their head.

**Sentence and phrase chunking** is the mechanism that makes this work. Instead of building sentences from isolated vocabulary and grammar rules at speaking time, learners internalize ready-made language units — whole sentences and native pre-assembled phrases — so retrieval feels automatic.

Chunking is how the product works, not what it sells. User-facing copy leads with the outcome (*"Speak without translating in your head"*), never with the mechanism.

## Learning approach

### Chunking method

Learners memorize **chunks**: fixed or semi-fixed expressions that native speakers use without conscious assembly.

Examples of chunk types:

- Full sentences: *"Could I get a table for two?"*
- Collocations and frames: *"I'm looking forward to …"*, *"It depends on …"*
- Situation-specific routines: greetings, ordering, travel, small talk

### Why chunks

| Traditional path | Chunking path |
|------------------|---------------|
| Word → grammar → assemble at runtime | Pattern → retrieve at runtime |
| High cognitive load while speaking | Lower load; focus on meaning and flow |
| Often sounds unnatural | Closer to native rhythm and phrasing |

### Recognition is not production

A learner who only reviews flashcards will recognize a sentence and still fail to say it. Every chunk must therefore be practiced in **production** modes — say it from L1, fill the missing part, swap a slot — not just recognized. This is why substitution drills sit at the center of the product rather than at the edge.

### Fewer frames, more sentences

Chunks are not stored as a flat list. Each frame carries swappable slots, so roughly 20 frames generate hundreds of usable sentences:

```text
Could you tell me more about...
    ├── your research
    ├── the program
    └── the application process
```

**Learn fewer patterns. Say more things.**

## Target outcome

A learner who has built a personal chunk library can:

- Respond in common situations without translating word-by-word
- Swap slot patterns (*"I'd like a …"*, *"Do you have any …?"*) with new vocabulary
- Maintain practice through spaced repetition and portable exports (e.g. Anki)
